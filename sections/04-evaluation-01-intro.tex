\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

% Test generalization strengthens the mutation scores of existing test suites
% by exploring additional inputs within already-covered execution paths.
% Our approach automates the transformation from conventional unit tests
% to property-based tests, thereby reducing the manual effort
% that would otherwise be required to perform such a transformation.
We evaluate the benefits, costs, and limitations
of our semantics-based test generalization approach
through the following seven research questions:

\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{RQ0:}] How do \ToolTeralizer{} and JARVIS compare in applicability and value coverage?
  \item[\textbf{RQ1:}] How much does test generalization improve the mutation score of existing unit test suites?
  \item[\textbf{RQ2:}] How does constraint complexity affect constraint-aware versus random input generation?
  \item[\textbf{RQ3:}] To which degree does generalization affect the size and runtime of the target test suites?
  \item[\textbf{RQ4:}] How efficient is test generalization compared to test generation?
  \item[\textbf{RQ5:}] What are the causes of unsuccessful generalization attempts under controlled conditions?
  \item[\textbf{RQ6:}] What are the causes of unsuccessful generalization attempts under real-world conditions?
\end{description}

Section~\ref{sec:experimental-framework} describes our experimental setup.
Sections~\ref{sec:jarvis-comparison}--\ref{sec:limitations-eval-extended} present the results for RQ0--RQ6.
All experiments ran on a MacBook Air (M2, 24~GB RAM) with default JVM settings,
and all data is available in our replication package~\cite{replicationpackage}.
