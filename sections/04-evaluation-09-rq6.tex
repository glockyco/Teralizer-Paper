\subsection{RQ6: What are the causes of unsuccessful generalization attempts under real-world conditions?}
\label{sec:limitations-eval-extended}

RQ5 established exclusion causes under controlled conditions.
The version 7 evaluation initially selected \TzRealworldSelectedProjects{} RepoReapers projects.
The initial gate excluded \TzRealworldInitialGateExcludedProjects{},
and \TzRealworldNoExecutedTestExcludedProjects{} further projects had no executed tests,
leaving \TzRealworldEligibleProjects{} eligible projects.
RQ6 examines how \ToolTeralizer{} performs on this corpus
(Section~\ref{sec:experimental-framework})
to identify generalization barriers in real-world projects.
Section~\ref{sec:project-level-exclusions-extended} covers project-level exclusions.
Section~\ref{sec:test-assertion-generalization-exclusions-extended} examines
test, assertion, and generalization exclusions,
comparing exclusion rates to controlled settings.
Full project-level exclusions and
partial test, assertion, and generalization exclusions are interconnected:
when filtering or failures exclude all tests, assertions, or generalizations in a project,
the project is excluded.
Understanding exclusion patterns guides future work toward addressing the most impactful limitations.

\paragraph{Test-level Exclusions}%
\label{par:real-world-test-exclusions}

Of the 85,368 real-world tests in the selected RepoReapers projects,
44,875 (52.6\%) remain included (Table~\ref{tab:exclusions-breakdown-extended}),
compared with 83.1\% under controlled conditions (Table~\ref{tab:exclusions-breakdown}).
Filtering excludes 47.1\% of the tests, whereas processing failures exclude only 0.3\%.
This shows that known applicability restrictions of the current prototype,
rather than unexpected processing failures, account for almost all test-level exclusions.

The \texttt{NoAssertions} filter is the most prominent test-level barrier.
It rejects 24,266 real-world tests (32.9\%),
compared with 10.3\% under controlled conditions,
because \ToolTeralizer{} finds no assertion in the analyzed test method.
We reviewed a stratified sample of 100 tests, grouping
projects by how many \texttt{NoAssertions} rejections they contributed. After weighting the groups
to the full rejection population, we estimate that
\TzRqsixReconstructionNoAssertionsGenuineAbsenceEstimatePct{} of rejected tests genuinely contain
no assertion (95\% CI: \TzRqsixReconstructionNoAssertionsGenuineAbsenceCiLowerPct{}--
\TzRqsixReconstructionNoAssertionsGenuineAbsenceCiUpperPct{}). Most rejections therefore reflect
limits in oracle recognition, such as assertions delegated to helper methods
or expressed in some other form that the current intraprocedural analysis does not recognize.

The \texttt{NonPassingTest} filter rejects \TzRealworldNonPassingRejectedTests{} methods
(10.5\%), compared with 6.6\% under controlled conditions. \ToolPit{} requires a green test suite
but permits exclusions only at the test-class level. \ToolTeralizer{} must therefore reject every
method in a class that contains a non-passing test. Of the rejected methods,
\TzRealworldNonPassingNonpassed{} (\TzRealworldNonPassingNonpassedPct{}) have at least one non-passing original execution, whereas
\TzRealworldNonPassingPassed{} (\TzRealworldNonPassingPassedPct{}) pass every execution and
are excluded only through class-level propagation. Many non-passing executions reflect
missing dependencies or unavailable services rather than incorrect assertions. Consequently, this
filter measures both unsuitable executions and collateral exclusions imposed by the mutation testing
interface.

The remaining filter exclusions identify narrower structural and tool limitations. The \texttt{Mocking}
filter rejects 6,973 tests because \ToolSPF{} cannot execute the mocking frameworks used in these tests,
thus preventing the collection of required input/output specifications.
A total of \TzRealworldTestFilteringInheritedMethodRejected{} + 1,053 tests are excluded
because they rely on specific inheritance structures that are not supported by the current prototype.
\texttt{TestType} shows only 199 rejections. It primarily captures unsupported execution models
or declaration-resolution limitations (e.g., \texttt{@Theory} annotations).
Together, these results show that secondary test exclusions arise primarily
from compatibility limitations and unsupported inheritance structures.

\paragraph{Assertion-level Exclusions}%
\label{par:real-world-assertion-exclusions}

Only \TzRealworldAssertionsIncluded{} of \TzRealworldAssertionsTotal{} real-world assertions
from the selected RepoReapers projects remain included (\TzRealworldAssertionsIncludedPct{}),
compared with 47.8\% under controlled conditions (Tables~\ref{tab:exclusions-breakdown-extended} and~\ref{tab:exclusions-breakdown}).
Filtering excludes 92.3\% of real-world assertions, while processing failures exclude 3.9\%.
All six assertion filters are applied to all \TzRealworldAssertionsTotal{} identified assertions,
so a single assertion can be rejected by multiple filters.

The \texttt{MissingValue} filter has the largest rejection rate: 39.1\% in RepoReapers, compared
with 24.7\% under controlled conditions. It rejects an assertion when tested method identification
cannot identify a MUT or obtain the source declaration and method signature needed for specification
extraction (Section~\ref{sec:tested-method-identification}). Of its
\TzRealworldMissingValueRejected{} rejections,
\TzRealworldMissingValueUnsupportedAssertionShape{} involve unsupported assertions,
\TzRealworldMissingValueLibraryDeclaration{} resolve to library declarations,
\TzRealworldMissingValueUnresolvedSourceDeclaration{} identify source declarations that Spoon
cannot resolve, and \TzRealworldMissingValueNoVisibleCall{} contain no visible call from which to
identify a MUT. The remaining \TzRealworldMissingValueMissingTestedFile{} lack the tested source
file. These causes distinguish unsupported assertions from limitations in source resolution and
tested method identification.

The \texttt{ParameterType} filter rejects 36.5\% of real-world assertions, compared with 15.4\%
under controlled conditions. \TzRealworldParameterTypeUnsupportedTypes{} rejections affect MUTs
that declare one or more parameters, but none of those parameters has a supported type. A
further \TzRealworldParameterTypeZeroParameters{} reject parameterless MUTs
that offer no opportunities for generalization.
For \TzRealworldParameterTypeMissingDeclaration{} rejections,
the selected call resolves to a library declaration or an unresolved source declaration,
so the MUT signature is unavailable. These assertions are also rejected by \texttt{MissingValue}.
The remaining \TzRealworldParameterTypeDerivationGap{} select a MUT
with a supported parameter but yield no generalizable input from the observed expression.
Thus, the largest recorded \texttt{ParameterType} cause is limited input-type
support, particularly for objects, collections, and arrays.

Limited type support also appears in the \texttt{ReturnType} and
\texttt{StringOperation} results. Of the \TzRealworldReturnTypeEvaluated{} assertions with return
type information,
\texttt{ReturnType} rejects \TzRealworldReturnTypeRejected{}
(\TzRealworldReturnTypeRejectedPct{})
because \ToolTeralizer{}'s oracle generalization does not support the return type of the corresponding MUT.
Commonly rejected types include \texttt{void}, collections, arrays, \texttt{java.lang.Object}, and
project-specific object types. \texttt{StringOperation} separately rejects 5,203 assertions
whose MUTs use string operations that the current \ToolSPF{}/\ToolTeralizer{}
implementation cannot model soundly (Section~\ref{sec:specification-extraction}). These results
expose the limits of specification extraction for broader string fragments, arrays, and objects.

The \texttt{AssertionType} filter rejects 17.5\% of real-world assertions,
compared with 2.6\% under controlled conditions.
The current implementation supports the assertions
and semantic contexts described in Section~\ref{sec:test-assertion-analysis}.
The most frequently rejected assertions are
\texttt{assertNotNull} (\TzRealworldAssertionTypeAssertNotNull{}),
\texttt{fail} (\TzRealworldAssertionTypeFail{}), \texttt{assertNull}
(\TzRealworldAssertionTypeAssertNull{}), and \texttt{assertThat}
(\TzRealworldAssertionTypeAssertThat{}).
Extending support for these assertions
requires both modeling of the corresponding assertion semantics
and improved type support for the values that these assertions compare.

Finally, \texttt{ExcludedTest} rejects an assertion whose test has already been excluded. This
affects 21.5\% of RepoReapers assertions, compared with 5.5\% under controlled conditions.
Its higher rejection rate therefore reflects the increase in test-level exclusions.

\paragraph{Generalization-level Exclusions}%
\label{par:real-world-generalization-exclusions}

Of the \TzRealworldGeneralizationAttempts{} generalization candidates in the RepoReapers projects,
\TzRealworldGeneralizationsValidated{} (\TzRealworldGeneralizationValidatedPct{})
produce a generalized test for which compilation, execution, report collection,
and the \texttt{NonPassingTest} filter all succeed.
Generalization-level filter decisions are sequential: each decision evaluates
only the generalization attempts or generalized tests that remain after earlier decisions.
The row populations in Table~\ref{tab:exclusions-filtering-extended}
therefore differ and must not be interpreted as overlapping decisions over one fixed population.

The \texttt{SeedSpecConsistency} gate rejects one attempt because its original concrete input does
not satisfy the extracted path condition. The \texttt{WideningLicense} gate then rejects
\TzRealworldWideningRefusals{} of the remaining attempts
(\TzRealworldWideningRefusalsPct{}). These widening refusals prevent \ToolTeralizer{} from applying
the extracted oracle to new inputs when the extracted path condition and symbolic output expression
do not provide sufficient evidence that the oracle remains valid throughout the input partition
(Section~\ref{sec:generalized-test-creation}).

Most widening refusals occur because the analysis records only a concrete return value,
with neither a symbolic expression relating that value to the inputs
nor evidence that the executed path returns a fixed literal:
\TzRealworldWideningRefusalOutputNotLiteral{} refusals, or
\TzRealworldWideningRefusalOutputNotLiteralPct{} of all refusals. Another
\TzRealworldWideningRefusalConcretization{} refusals
(\TzRealworldWideningRefusalConcretizationPct{}) occur
after a native operation receives a symbolic argument
and loses its symbolic relation to that argument
because SPF does not provide a symbolic model for the operation.
In \TzRealworldWideningRefusalParametersEmpty{} cases,
the path condition contains none of the generalizable parameters,
and in a further \TzRealworldWideningRefusalPathCoverage{} cases
it does not cover every generalizable parameter.
The remaining 14 refusals concern exception oracles
whose concretization or path condition evidence
does not establish that the expected exception remains reachable
for the generalized inputs.
Widening refusals therefore primarily expose cases
where symbolic information is lost
or otherwise cannot be recovered during specification extraction.

\ToolTeralizer{} emits \TzRealworldGeneralizationsEmitted{} candidate generalized tests.
Compilation or processing failures prevent 22
from reaching the \texttt{NonPassingTest} filter
(Table~\ref{tab:exclusions-breakdown-extended}).
The filter then rejects 420 of the remaining
\TzRealworldGeneralizationsFilterResultRecorded{} tests
and includes \TzRealworldGeneralizationsFilterPassed{}.
At this post-generation boundary, RepoReapers projects
show inclusions for \TzRqsixFilteringRealworldIncludedPct{} of generalized tests,
compared with \TzRqsixFilteringControlledIncludedPct{} under controlled conditions.
\TzRealworldGeneralizationsFinalUsable{} generalized tests from
\TzRealworldFinalUsableProjects{} projects then complete Stage~5,
the mutation testing that supports test suite reduction.
